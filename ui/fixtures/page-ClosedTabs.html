Reading the sky before there were maps

By Ines Marlowe, 8 minute read

Long before charts and compasses, sailors crossed open water by memorising where stars rose and set. A single bright point near the horizon could hold a heading for hours.

Navigators learned a small set of guide stars and the order they appeared through the night. As one climbed too high to be useful, the next one took over.

The method was less about the stars themselves than about patience: the same sky, read the same way, night after night.
